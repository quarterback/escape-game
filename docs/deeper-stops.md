# Coalition: deeper stops, harder escape, phase turns

**Owner direction (October 30, 2026):**
- Turns work like a board game, with transitions between phases and more markers on screen.
- Each stop should feel different and hold real decisions.
- The escape should be much harder. Coordination falls apart, and the cost of a mistake is being caught: questioned, held at a station, placed under house arrest.
- Use the source articles, then invent wild counterfactuals for flavor and replay.
- Add a daily run that people can share, background sound, and real sprites with layered backgrounds.

## What the articles add

| From the reporting | How the game uses it |
| --- | --- |
| Passports had to be handed in within 24 hours. When they weren't, the passports were cancelled and an alert went to immigration. | **The deadline.** At Monday 12:00 your own passport is cancelled and the ban reaches every post. Holds escalate faster after that. |
| Police said the travel ban did not make him a fugitive or allow his arrest. | **A ladder of holds, not an instant loss.** You can be stopped and questioned, then held at a local station, then placed under house arrest. Only house arrest ends the run, and even then you get one desperate try. |
| A convoy of four cars split up. Allies told reporters he had reached Florida while he was still in Brazil. Even close allies did not know where he was. | **Decoys and premature announcements.** They buy time, but they also leave your own people working in the dark. |
| A foreign official coordinated from the border town. The jet waited a day. The plane refueled in a third country. | **Arrangements can stall,** and the flight itself can still go wrong after takeoff is booked. |
| The father was under house detention. The brother did the coordinating from abroad. | **Family is leverage.** Sosā can press the sibling, and family members create problems you have to manage. |

## Systems

### Phase turns (escape side)

Each round has five phases. A short full-screen card announces each round and phase.

1. **News.** A dispatch event, or a hold, shown as a card with two or three choices. Each choice shows its cost as tags (hours, exposure, roll, cards, trust), not as sentences.
2. **Phone.** One phone action: answer a message or place a call. Or skip.
3. **Move.** One approach at this stop, or lie low.
4. **Roll.** The die, the outcome and what changed, shown as tags.
5. **Sosā.** What the inquiry did during the hours that passed, and any new hold.

### Events

- One event is drawn on arrival at each stop, and another every six hours spent there.
- Each stop has its own pool, and a shared pool can come up anywhere.
- Events are counterfactuals and do not follow the real story: a strike, a blizzard, a wedding with three hundred guests, a tour bus to the waterfalls, a guard who once served in your ministry, your rival offering a deal.
- Some choices open routes that skip the expected approach. Some plant problems that come back later.

### The ladder of holds

| Rung | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Questioned | You lose two to four hours, and one card may be seized. |
| Held at a station | You lose about eight hours, and the card you would most want may be seized. Getting out needs a call, a bribe or a lawyer. |
| House arrest | The run ends, unless one final escape roll succeeds. |

You climb a rung when exposure reaches 10 (exposure then resets to 6), when Sosā searches the place you are actually in, or when someone betrays you. After the passport deadline, climbs come faster.

### People you might wrongly trust

- At the start of each run, two allies are secretly compromised. Which two changes every run.
- When you rely on a compromised ally, they may leak your location. Rumors are events that hint at who it might be.
- The markers show what you know about each person: trusted, unknown, doubted or talked. Testing someone costs time and reveals the truth.

### The two candidates differ

- **Teresā Albukerkē,** former interior minister. She starts with a friend inside the inquiry. Police know her face, so checks at the border are harder.
- **Matēu Zanetī,** shipping heir. He starts with the party machine and his shipping contacts, which open a river barge. He has been watched since the count: he starts with more exposure, and Sosā starts with the right address.

### Difficulty target

The calibration harness's simple policy won 59% before this pass. The target is roughly 20 to 35%, with holds causing more losses than the clock.

### Daily run

- The seed and the candidate come from the date, so everyone plays the same run that day.
- The result is shared through the phone's share menu, or copied as a short line of text.
